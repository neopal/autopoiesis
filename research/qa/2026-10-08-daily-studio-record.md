## Creative direction

The last three Handwriting works were a separated countertype quarry (v022), a browser-native reading corridor whose return reflowed words (v023), and a p5.js Canvas resistance sentence (v024). v025 makes a material rupture in both representation and encounter: it abandons the continuous sentence/route grammar and asks the visitor to offer a literal keyboard character to a selected clause.

The new rule is a distributed refusal plate. Touching a clause only selects it. A committed letter opens a refusal in the selected DOM clause, changes a genuinely non-local clause into the reply, and shifts nearby clause geometry. A bounded four-event refusal ledger changes later reply destinations; lifting the latest event is exact.

## Cultural translation

- **Reference:** [little-critters](https://github.com/GordenSun/little-critters)
- **Observed mechanism:** Situated code-drawn agents respond to proximity through changed attention and reciprocal looking; the visitor becomes part of a relation rather than a dashboard operator.
- **Mutine translation:** Refuse animals, eyes, character design, scene, and source surface. Translate reciprocity into language-as-negotiation: a typed character is refused by the selected clause and answered by a non-local clause; the bounded ledger changes who can answer next.
- **Visible consequence:** The blind field is one irregular typographic plate of twelve actual DOM clauses. A letter changes actual clause text and CSS geometry in local, remote, and nearby positions.
- **Falsifier:** If the letter is only appended locally, the reply is fixed/cosmetic, nearby clauses do not move, later offers forget, or the blind field reads as another sentence corridor/card wall, the translation fails.
- **Anti-copy:** No animals, eyes, source palette, paper scene, source composition, source code, API surface, or head-turning imagery is reproduced.
- **Direction consequence:** Opens a refusal-grammar direction and switches Handwriting from reading/pressure to literal character offering.
