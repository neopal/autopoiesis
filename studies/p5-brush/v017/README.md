# Brush v017 — proximity archipelago

## Status

**Candidate / held.** This is the 2026-09-23 daily tableau for the Brush current. It remains held until a caption-free comparison confirms that the sparse island field reads as a material relation rather than a decorative constellation.

## Hypothesis

If paint remembers removal as a relation, proximity should not merely move a cursor or tint a mark. It should make one material body yield, make another receive the displaced load, and leave a third body materially open.

## Repeated grammar refused

The last three Brush works (v014–v016) were route fields, then a basin field, then a singular tiled pressure plate. Their repeated gesture was passage or incision through a continuous or gridded surface. v017 refuses both: the first encounter is a sparse population of isolated material bodies with negative space between them.

## Changed rule

v017 translates situated attention into a material transfer. A visitor approaching the field selects the nearest island. That island contracts, opens a porous notch, and loses sediment; a distant island swells and receives the load; a third island hollows out. The event is carried by changed island geometry, not by a witness line or an explanatory marker.

## Visible consequence

The blind first render is a porous archipelago of thirteen separate painted bodies, with an intentionally open field between them. After interaction, one body shrinks and opens, one grows and turns, and one becomes visibly porous. Repeated autonomous events accumulate actual population memory. The latest lift reconstructs the exact prior island field.

## Interaction and reversibility

Move near an island, click, press Enter, or press Space to make one bounded proximity encounter. The nearest island becomes the source, a distant island becomes the receiver, and a third island becomes the vacancy. Delete/Backspace or “lift latest” removes only the latest encounter. `release sequence` clears visitor memory and restarts the autonomous sequence. The engine is pure, seeded, and replayable.

## Cultural translation

- **Reference:** `little-critters` — https://github.com/GordenSun/little-critters
- **Observed mechanism:** situated agents respond to pointer proximity by changing attention and looking back, making the visitor part of a reciprocal encounter rather than a dashboard operator.
- **Mutine translation:** remove agents, eyes, and social scene; make proximity redistribute actual painted material across three situated islands: yield, receive, open.
- **Visible consequence:** the nearest island loses radius and sediment and gains a porous notch; a distant island gains radius and sediment; a third island gains porosity and a true internal vacancy.
- **Falsifier:** if proximity only changes colour, if receiver growth is not visible, or if the vacancy is a dark sticker over unchanged geometry, the translation fails.
- **Anti-copy:** no animals, characters, paper style, head-turning scene, source palette, composition, or surface vocabulary is reproduced.
- **Direction consequence:** this closes the Brush current's route/basin/pressure-plate vocabulary and opens a population grammar: isolated material bodies, proximity as transfer, and absence as a shared social consequence.

## Falsifier

If a caption-free viewer reads a generic decorative blob field, if the source/receiver/vacancy relation is only explained by labels, if pointer movement is cosmetic, or if undo does not restore the exact prior geometry, v017 is a failed medium switch.

## Deletion condition

Delete v017 if a labels-off, controls-off, readout-off comparison still reads as v016's pressure plate or any earlier horizontal route field. Do not rescue the direction with annotations, witness lines, colour coding, or a new title.

## Determinism

Seed: `0x42525537`. Base islands, autonomous encounters, material transfer, and visitor selection are deterministic. Pointer coordinates are clamped and copied into bounded memory; repeated input and lift sequences are replayable.

This is a candidate / held daily work. Engine and source checks are evidence of structure, not proof of visual success. Independent caption-free perceptual review and production readback remain explicit gates.
