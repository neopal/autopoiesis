# Brush v019 — ordinal register

## Status

**Candidate / held.** This is the 2026-09-25 daily tableau for the Brush current. It remains held until a caption-free comparison confirms that the missing rung, the later sag, and the far counterweight read as one material memory rather than a diagram with decorative bars.

## Hypothesis

If paint remembers removal as load, a missing mark should not become a hole in a surface. It should become a load-bearing gap in an ordered register: the remaining pigment settles under the lost weight and the removed mass stays visible at the edge.

## Repeated grammar refused

The last three Brush works used a pressure plate, a population of islands, and a continuous membrane. Their repeated encounter was drawing, approach, or departure over a 2D field; their repeated visual grammar was pigment as a surface or surface-like body. v019 abandons the surface and the pointer encounter. It is a discrete hanging register with a wheel/keyboard task and a gravity-like settlement rule.

## Changed rule

v019 explicitly inverts v018's departure event. The visitor is no longer a presence whose absence opens a skin. A wheel turn advances an ordinal register and removes one actual pigment weight. The missing rung remains as a slot, every later weight sags under the missing load, and the removed mass becomes a counterweight at the far edge. The gap is the memory.

## Visible consequence

The blind first render is a horizontal rack of irregular vertical pigment weights, not a sheet or island field. After a turn, one weight is absent from the register, later weights have changed height/lean, and a new suspended counterweight appears at the edge. Repeated turns produce a readable sequence of absences; lifting the latest turn reconstructs the exact preceding register.

## Interaction and reversibility

Turn the wheel over the rack or press ArrowUp/ArrowDown to commit one bounded turn. Enter and Space repeat the positive turn for keyboard and assistive paths; Delete/Backspace or “lift latest” removes only the latest turn. “Release register” clears visitor memory and restarts the autonomous sequence. The engine is pure, seeded, and replayable.

## Cultural translation

- **Reference:** `little-critters` — https://github.com/GordenSun/little-critters
- **Observed mechanism:** situated agents change attention through proximity and look back, making the visitor part of a reciprocal encounter rather than a dashboard operator.
- **Mutine translation:** invert both reciprocity and proximity in the Brush current: the visitor becomes an ordinal metronome. A discrete wheel turn, not a gaze or departure, removes one weight; the absence changes later load and reappears as a counterweight.
- **Visible consequence:** the work switches from continuous surface to hanging register, from pointer presence to wheel/keyboard step, and from hole/back-fold to gap/sag/counterweight.
- **Falsifier:** if a wheel turn does not remove a real rung, if later weights do not sag, or if the counterweight is only a label or colour shift, the translation fails.
- **Anti-copy:** no animals, characters, paper style, head-turning scene, source palette, composition, or surface vocabulary is reproduced.
- **Direction consequence:** closes Brush v016's pressure cut, v017's archipelago transfer, and v018's departure fold; opens a discrete load-memory direction in which absence changes the order and weight of later material.

## Falsifier

If a caption-free viewer sees only a decorative set of bars, if the wheel merely changes a counter, if the gap is painted over rather than structural, or if undo fails to restore the exact prior register, v019 is a failed direction change.

## Deletion condition

Delete v019 if labels-off, controls-off viewing still reads as a surface, island field, or back-fold, or if the missing rung and sagging sequence cannot be found without the annotations. Do not rescue the load-bearing gap with explanatory prose.

## Determinism

Seed: `0x42525539`. Base rack, autonomous turns, rung selection, counterweight placement, and visitor turn directions are deterministic. No pointer coordinate participates in the visual state. Repeated turn → turn → lift sequences are replayable.

## Quality boundary

This is a candidate / held daily work. Engine and source checks are evidence of structure, not proof of visual success. Independent caption-free perceptual review and production readback remain explicit gates.
