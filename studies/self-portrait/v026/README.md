# Self portrait v026 — The portrait elects an absence.

## Hypothesis

A self-portrait can portray its own decisions by separating the visitor's local attention from the place where the portrait changes. The work should not answer the visitor's gaze at the visited mark; it should remember exposure as a social field and elect an unseen mark to become absent.

## Changed rule

v026 abandons v025's wide decision ribbon, v024's self-measuring SVG membrane, and v023's single observed Canvas solid. The medium becomes a semantic DOM witness field: fifteen irregular marks form one scattered manuscript rather than a canvas object or a route. Visiting a mark only increases its local exposure. A committed witness makes the least-seen *other* mark become an actual absence, makes the most exposed remote mark reply, and gives a third mark the counter-decision. Four elections remain in bounded memory; lifting the latest reconstructs the prior field exactly.

## Cultural translation

- **Reference:** [little-critters](https://github.com/GordenSun/little-critters)
- **Observed mechanism:** a population of code-drawn agents responds to pointer proximity with situated attention and reciprocal looking, making the visitor part of a social field without a dashboard.
- **Mutine translation:** refuse animals, eyes, and the look-back scene. Translate situated reciprocity into a non-figurative witness field: local exposure is stored, but commitment is answered by a remote absence election rather than by the visited mark.
- **Visible consequence:** the blind view is a scattered semantic manuscript of fifteen abstract marks. After commitment, one unseen mark opens a physical gap, a remote mark stretches and turns, and a third mark carries a counter-shear; later elections inherit exposure memory.
- **Anti-copy:** no animals, character designs, paper scene, source palette, composition, API surface, or head-turning image is reproduced.

## Visible consequence

The first render is a dark, spacious field of irregular cream and violet marks connected by faint threads. It is neither a face nor a card array. A committed witness changes three actual DOM marks: the elected absence is shorter and cut, the remote reply elongates and twists, and a counter mark slips out of register. The untouched visited mark remains a witness, not a target.

## Interaction

- Visit a mark with pointer, touch, or keyboard focus; proximity and the first witness do not alter geometry.
- `Enter` / `Space` or **witness a mark** commits the deterministic absence election.
- `Delete` lifts the latest election and reconstructs the previous field.
- `R` or **release the field** clears bounded memory.
- `S` downloads the current deterministic state as JSON.

## Falsifier

The translation fails if the visited mark is always the one that changes, if visiting changes geometry before commitment, if the reply is only colour or a readout, if later elections ignore exposure, or if the blind field reads as a generic dashboard or card grid.

## Deletion condition

Delete v026 if the blind field cannot show a material absence, a remote reply, and a counter-decision without labels or prose; if semantic DOM geometry is only decorative; or if the work collapses into the ribbon, membrane, or solid-object grammar it was meant to leave.

## Direction consequence

This opens a self-portrait branch in which code represents its own decision by distributing agency across an attention field. It closes the direct-target / departure-as-causality family and makes the next mutation test whether a field can refuse even the act of commitment without becoming inert.

## Evidence boundary

This candidate is held pending independent caption-free perceptual comparison, full local and production browser matrices, deployment readback, and provider revision linkage. The artifact and deterministic engine are real; those remaining gates are not implied by this record.
