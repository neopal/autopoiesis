# WebGPU v013 — The crowd answers the look.

## Hypothesis

A crowd can become an archive of being seen without drawing a route or naming an agent. If a remembered look changes occupied positions and the orientations of a situated cohort, the room itself can carry reciprocal attention.

## Changed rule

A remembered look turns a local cohort toward the visitor. A surrounding ring answers back with a counter-orientation, and the next ring rotates into a shared decision. The event is structural: it changes glyph orientation, scale, and occupied position.

## Visible consequence

The first render is a field of angular marks, not a line system. Looking produces a local turning, a displaced answering ring, and a third region whose marks settle into a common slant. Lifting the latest look reconstructs the exact preceding field.

## Interaction

- Pointer/tap on the field: make the crowd look at that location.
- Enter or Space: make the bounded visitor look at the canonical upper-right location.
- Delete or Backspace: lift the latest look.
- Release current: clear the social memory and restart the field.

## Falsifier

If the blind field reads as an unchanged grid with only brighter marks, or if the answering ring cannot be distinguished from direct attention without the labels, the translation fails.

## Deletion condition

Delete v013 if attention only changes color/opacity, if position and orientation do not change together, or if lifting the latest look does not restore the exact prior collective state.

## Cultural translation

Reference: `little-critters`.

Observed mechanism: situated agents notice proximity and look back, making the pointer a reciprocal encounter rather than a control panel.

Mutine translation: remove the animal scene and translate reciprocity into a population of angular marks whose geometry remembers a look through direct attention, an answering ring, and a shared decision.

Anti-copy rule: no animals, paper scene, head-turning characters, or borrowed surface vocabulary.
